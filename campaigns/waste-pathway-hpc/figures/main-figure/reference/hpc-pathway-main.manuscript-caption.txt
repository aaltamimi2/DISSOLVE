Figure X. Agent-driven techno-economic and life-cycle optimization of a mixed plastic film. Top: an illustrative query and the supported tool workflow. The pathway tool screens for selective solvents, costs each admissible wash by process simulation (BioSTEAM), enumerates all routes of zero to three washes followed by a destination for the residual, and returns the exact Pareto fronts; a second tool reports the sensitivity of a single wash. Middle: cost of one wash, shown for LDPE dissolved in toluene with on-site heat and power (C1). Left, minimum selling price (MSP) of the recovered polymer as a function of the LDPE content of the feed and plant capacity (152 simulated plants; logarithmic colour scale). Centre and right, the four largest total-order Sobol indices for MSP and for global warming potential (GWP), computed separately in the campaign from a global sensitivity analysis over 17 process and economic parameters (N = 1,024; whiskers, 95% bootstrap intervals). Bottom left: annual profit against gate-to-gate emissions for the 324 candidate routes for the example film (60% LDPE, 20% PET, 10% nylon 6, 10% EVOH) in a dedicated 8,000 t/year plant. The line marks the Pareto front and its three routes; two candidates above 22 kt CO₂e/year are not shown. Bottom right: extra profit from deeper separation, ΔP = Pₘ − Pₛ, where Pₘ is the highest profit among routes with two or three washes and Pₛ the highest among routes with zero or one, per tonne of feed. Each point is one of 300 joint draws of prices and process parameters, for the same 8,000 t/year feed processed in a dedicated plant or charged its throughput share of costs in a shared 20,000 t/year plant; diamonds and bars show the median and 5th–95th percentiles. Routes that sell the washed residual as resin were excluded throughout because its purity is not established. All values are model predictions; emissions carry no credit for displaced virgin resin.
